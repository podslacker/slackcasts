Building a Reliable Agent – From Prototype to Production

Session: Google Cloud Next 2024 – AI Track
Speakers: Alibek (Engineering Manager, Booking.com AI Platform), Maria (Software Engineer, Booking.com AI Platform), Manasa Kandula (Application Modernization Specialist, Google), Naz Bayraktar (AI Specialist, Google)

Overview

The talk walks through Booking.com’s journey of turning experimental generative‑AI (GenAI) prototypes into a governed, production‑ready, multi‑provider AI ecosystem. It showcases several real‑world agentic use cases, the technical lessons learned when serving large language models (LLMs) on Google Cloud (Vertex AI), and then hands the stage to Google experts who present four practical principles for evaluating, deploying, securing, and observing agents at scale.

3️⃣ Technical Learnings – Serving LLMs on Vertex AI

  1. One Size Does Not Fit All

    • On‑Demand (default) → insufficient latency & scalability for Know Before You Go.
    • Provisioned Throughput → reserved token capacity; better but still limited.
    • Priority Processing → needed for multimodal workloads like Reels to Reality.

  2. Match Serving Tier to Use‑Case

    • Determine required throughput, latency, and multimodal support before choosing a tier.

  3. Governance & Visibility

    • Central gateway with built‑in usage monitoring, safety guardrails, and model‑agnostic APIs.
    • Platform team provides self‑service docs, hackathons, and up‑to‑date tutorials to move teams from experimentation to production.

Quote: “Building the platform is only half the job; the other half is helping users understand what’s available and how to start.” – Maria

4️⃣ Google’s Four Principles for Production Agents

Principle What It Solves Practical Tips
Evaluation Non‑deterministic behavior makes QA hard. • Response evaluation (LLM‑as‑judge, automated metrics: hallucination, groundedness, coherence).
• Trajectory evaluation – inspect tool calls, parameters, and agent collaboration.
Rapid Cloud Deployment Moving from prototype code to scalable service. Use Vertex AI Agent Engine, Model Armor, and CI/CD pipelines to spin up serving endpoints quickly.
Identity & Security at Deploy Prevent misuse, ensure provenance. Assign service‑account‑based identities, enforce IAM policies, and enable model‑level access controls.
Observability & Auditing Debugging & compliance in production. Leverage Cloud Logging, Trace, Metrics, and Audit logs; instrument agents to emit tool‑call traces for post‑mortem analysis.

Quote: “Agents are non‑deterministic, so you need both response‑level and trajectory‑level evaluation.” – Naz Bayraktar

5️⃣ Takeaways

  1. Start Small, Iterate Fast – Early prototypes give insight, but a unified gateway with governance is essential for scaling.
  2. Choose the Right Serving Tier – Evaluate latency, throughput, and multimodal needs before locking into a Vertex AI tier.
  3. Governance is Not an After‑thought – Built‑in safety guardrails, usage monitoring, and model‑level controls keep the ecosystem trustworthy.
  4. Evaluation Must Be Multi‑Dimensional – Combine human review, LLM‑as‑judge, and automated trajectory metrics.
  5. Enable Self‑Service for Teams – Documentation, tutorials, and community events (hackathons) accelerate adoption across a large organization.

Booking.com transformed experimental GenAI projects into a governed, production‑grade AI platform powered by Google’s Gemini and Vertex AI.
