📌 Quick Action Checklist

  1. Set up automated reminders to trigger renewal review calls 45‑60 days before each policy’s renewal date.
  2. Create a standard review script covering market outlook, coverage gaps, and cross‑sell options.
  3. Design a cancellation call framework: ask why?, explore alternatives, offer payment/coverage tweaks before confirming the cancel.
  4. Build a retention training program – weekly role‑plays, shadow sessions with top performers, script libraries.

Think of each renewal as a relationship checkpoint, a moment to reinforce trust and uncover new opportunities.

JORDAN: Strategy number one is all about proactive renewal review calls. Instead of waiting for a client to pick up the phone and say “I’m out,” you reach out—ideally 45 to 60 days before the policy expires. This call isn’t a price‑only pitch; it’s a conversation about market conditions, coverage gaps, and how you can protect them better.

JORDAN: By framing the call as a risk‑management review, you position yourself as a strategic partner, not just a salesperson. That mindset opens the door to cross‑selling—turning a monoline client into a multi‑line relationship, and the data shows multi‑line clients tend to stick around longer.

JORDAN: On to strategy two: handling cancellations proactively. If a client calls to cancel, pause before you process the request. Ask “Why are you leaving?”—you might discover it’s a misunderstanding about coverage, a recent life change, or even a pricing concern you can address on the spot.

JORDAN: Even if they ultimately decide to leave, the way you respond can leave a lasting impression. A respectful, solution‑focused exit can turn a lost client into a future referral—or even a comeback down the line. Remember, every interaction is a brand touchpoint.

JORDAN: The third pillar is building a team of retention rock stars. Retention isn’t luck; it’s a learnable skill. Equip your reps with scripts, role‑play tough renewal scenarios, and let them shadow your top performers. When they see real‑world success, confidence follows.

JORDAN: Role‑playing isn’t just a training exercise; it’s a rehearsal for real conversations. Have reps practice objection handling, payment option discussions, and coverage adjustments until the dialogue feels natural. The more fluent they become, the more likely they’ll save an account.

JORDAN: Empowering your team also means giving them ownership of the process. When reps feel they’re truly advocating for the client’s best interest, they’ll push harder to find creative solutions—whether that’s a flexible payment plan or a tailored coverage tweak.

JORDAN: If you’re wondering where to start, there are plenty of resources out there—from on‑demand video libraries to live virtual training sessions that bring reps from across the country together. Choose the format that fits your agency’s rhythm, but make sure it’s consistent and actionable.

JORDAN: To recap, the playbook is simple: schedule proactive renewal calls, turn cancellation moments into discovery calls, and train your reps to be retention champions. Each step reduces churn, deepens client relationships, and ultimately lifts revenue.
